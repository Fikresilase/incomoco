# Cash flow: money available to run your business

Cash flow is the movement of money into and out of a business. Cash coming in may be money customers pay you. Cash going out includes payments for stock, rent, transport, and other business costs.

Cash balance is the money available at a particular time. Opening cash plus cash received minus cash paid equals closing cash. For example, a shop starts the week with 1,000 birr. Customers pay 3,000 birr during the week. The owner pays 2,500 birr for stock, rent, and transport. Closing cash is 1,000 plus 3,000 minus 2,500, which equals 1,500 birr.

Profit and cash are different. A sale on credit can contribute to profit, but the customer has not yet paid cash. A business can be profitable and still be short of cash when bills are due.

A simple cash record lists the date, description, cash received, cash paid, and running balance. Record actual cash movements regularly. Keep business and personal money separate. Check which payments are coming due and which customers are expected to pay. This helps you plan before a shortage happens.
